\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proposed Value-Grounded AI Alignment and the Value-Grounded Transformer Architecture as a testable research framework. The design separates a stable core axiology, a context-sensitive normative ontology, runtime-bound purpose and domain semantics, and evidence-backed world state. It further combines possible structural participation in Transformer computation with independent formal assurance at consequential action boundaries.

The central proposition is modest: explicit semantic value structure may act as an inductive bias for alignment-relevant computation rather than appearing only as training supervision, natural-language policy, or inference-time guardrail. The proposition may fail. The proposed ablations, metrics, threat model, and falsification conditions are intended to make that failure visible.

The repository's executable component is correspondingly narrow. It parses illustrative ontology fixtures, exposes future-model interfaces, runs transparent synthetic action-selection scenarios, and computes deterministic descriptive uncertainty summaries. It does not train a Transformer, demonstrate alignment, establish moral truth, or provide a production verifier. The next research phase should build a governed dataset and implement the smallest matched auxiliary-loss experiment, with preregistered controls and adversarial evaluation, before adding routing or self-modifying ontology mechanisms.
