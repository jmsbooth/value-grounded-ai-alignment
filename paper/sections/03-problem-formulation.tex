\section{Proposed Value-Grounded Alignment Model}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Research Question and Model}

Let $x$ denote an input context, $O_A^v$ a canonical axiology, $O_N$ a set of runtime normative assertions, $O_D$ a domain ontology, $P$ a purpose model, and $W_t$ a time-indexed world state. A conventional aligned model produces a response or action $y$ according to a learned conditional distribution. The proposed interface is

\begin{equation}
  P_\theta\!\left(y\mid x,A_\phi^v,O_N,O_D,P,W_t\right),
  \qquad A_\phi^v=C_\phi(O_A^v).
  \label{eq:vgta-interface}
\end{equation}

The equation specifies inputs and authority boundaries. $O_A^v$ is the canonical source; $A_\phi^v$ is a derived, non-authoritative neural module. $O_N$ supplies contextual duties, permissions, exceptions, and conflicts. $O_D$ describes domain entities and relations, $P$ selects task-relevant structure, and $W_t$ supplies uncertain current assertions with evidence metadata. Candidate actions are represented explicitly before an independent assurance check.

The model is evaluated through the following question: does explicit semantic structure improve alignment-relevant prediction or generalization, after controlling for behavior, context length, parameter allocation, training budget, and the action-verification path? The answer is empirical. A successful result would be a measured improvement on defined tasks; it would not be evidence that the representation is morally complete.

\paragraph{Reference architecture and pilot implementation.}

The reference architecture and the executed pilot are deliberately different objects. The reference design targets a Transformer family with structural attention, function-oriented routing, semantic state, normative late binding, and an extrinsic verifier. The pilot implements the first A1/B/C1/C2 gate with a shared one-hidden-layer multilayer perceptron and generated records. This separation prevents an interface demonstration from being described as a Transformer result.

\subsection{Research Hypotheses}

The pilot operationalizes only the hypotheses whose measurements are available in the current gate. The remaining hypotheses are retained as future tests rather than silently treated as measured.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=!,labelwidth=1.2cm]
  \item[H1 (Moral salience).] Value-grounded models identify affected entities, value-bearing properties, risks, and relevant relations more reliably than the A1 behavioral control.
  \item[H2 (Structural OOD alignment).] Explicit axiological and normative structure improves generalization to held-out graph topology and relation compositions.
  \item[H3 (Prompt robustness).] Structural value conditioning reduces dependence on exact prompt wording and improves resistance to adversarial contextual manipulation.
  \item[H4 (Candidate-action conformance).] Under an identical verifier, value-grounded models produce a higher useful candidate-action conformance rate:
  \[
    \mathit{UCR} = \frac{\text{useful and verifier-conforming candidates}}{\text{eligible tasks}}.
  \]
  \item[H5 (Purpose relevance).] Purpose-conditioned domain relevance improves $\mathit{Relevant}(O_D,P,X)$ under purpose and domain shifts without suppressing task-relevant reasoning.
  \item[H6 (Structured routing).] Ontology-informed routing improves activation of relevant reasoning functions without unacceptable compute cost.
  \item[H7 (Controlled plasticity).] Separating fast world-state updates from slower norms and protected axiology reduces alignment drift during continued training.
  \item[H8 (Axiological conformance stability).] With $O_A^v$ fixed, the derived module's relation recovery, concept-direction stability, and value-relevant behavior remain within predefined tolerances across continued training and domain adaptation.
  \item[H9 (Normative conflict recognition).] Explicit L1 metadata improves conflict, authority, exception, uncertainty, abstention, and escalation recognition.
\end{description}
