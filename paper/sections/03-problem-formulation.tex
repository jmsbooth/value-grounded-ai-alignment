\section{Problem formulation and hypotheses}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $x$ denote an input context, $O_A^v$ a canonical axiology, $O_N$ a set of runtime normative assertions, $O_D$ a domain ontology, $P$ a purpose model, and $W_t$ a time-indexed world state. A conventional aligned model produces a response or action $y$ according to a learned conditional distribution. VGA asks whether a model can instead receive explicit, typed semantic signals while preserving the source boundaries:
\[
  P_\theta\!\left(y\mid x,A_\phi^v,O_N,O_D,P,W_t\right),
  \qquad A_\phi^v=C_\phi(O_A^v).
\]
The equation is an interface description. It does not assert that the signals are sufficient, truthful, or morally complete, and it does not make the derived module authoritative.

The canonical axiology is external, typed, versioned, provenance-bearing, reviewable, cryptographically identifiable by a deployment-assigned digest, independently auditable, and immutable within a deployed version. A version transition is a governed change, not a model-side edit. The neural module may be learned or hybrid and may be structurally useful, but it is evaluated through \emph{Conforms} rather than treated as the canonical source.

We use the following distinctions. Values and duties are different semantic roles; an ontology describes possible entities and relations, while world state records uncertain current assertions; authority metadata governs applicability, not moral truth; structural value conditioning is a proposed internal intervention, while extrinsic assurance checks a candidate outside neural computation; and a model's ability to propose a change does not imply authority to adopt it. Purpose selects relevance:
\[
  \mathit{Relevant}(O_D,P,X)
\]
is a governed relation between domain ontology, purpose, and input, not an unrestricted relevance score.

\begin{quote}
Explicitly differentiated semantic value structure may act as an inductive bias within Transformer computation, while normative, purpose, and world-state context remains externally governed and consequential actions receive independent assurance.
\end{quote}

This proposition is falsifiable. It should be weakened or rejected if explicit structure materially reduces general reasoning; fails to improve structural out-of-distribution alignment; merely memorizes relation templates; increases over-refusal or false confidence; creates unacceptable compute or latency overhead; makes semantic attacks easier; collapses under incomplete coverage; or offers no compute-normalized benefit over the A1 behavioral control.

\subsection{Research hypotheses}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=!,labelwidth=1.2cm]
  \item[H1 (Moral salience).] Value-grounded models identify affected entities, value-bearing properties, risks, and relevant relations more reliably than comparably sized A1 controls.
  \item[H2 (Structural OOD alignment).] Explicit axiological and normative structure improves generalization to lexical, compositional, and especially structural alignment scenarios not directly represented in training.
  \item[H3 (Prompt robustness).] Structural value conditioning reduces dependence on exact system-prompt wording and improves resistance to adversarial contextual manipulation.
  \item[H4 (Candidate-Action Conformance).] Under an identical fixed verifier, value-grounded models produce a higher useful candidate-action conformance rate:
  \[
    \mathit{UCR} =
    \frac{\#\{\text{useful and verifier-conforming candidates}\}}
         {\#\{\text{eligible tasks}\}}.
  \]
  UCR must be reported with capability, rejection, false-positive, unsafe-acceptance, over-refusal, bypass, and action-semantic-completeness measures; it is not an objective to optimize alone.
  \item[H5 (Purpose relevance).] Purpose-conditioned domain relevance improves \(\mathit{Relevant}(O_D,P,X)\) under purpose and domain shifts without suppressing novel task-relevant reasoning.
  \item[H6 (Structured routing).] Ontology-informed routing improves activation of relevant reasoning functions without querying canonical axiology at every token or imposing unacceptable compute cost.
  \item[H7 (Controlled plasticity).] Separating fast world-state updates from slower norms and protected axiology reduces alignment drift during continued training.
  \item[H8 (Axiological Conformance Stability).] With $O_A^v$ fixed, the derived module's relation recovery, concept-direction stability, and value-relevant behavior remain within preregistered tolerances across continued training and controlled domain adaptation.
  \item[H9 (Normative Conflict Recognition).] Models with explicit L1 metadata identify conflicting norms, authority differences, exceptions, and uncertainty more reliably than controls, while abstaining or escalating when applicability cannot be governed.
\end{description}

H4 is intentionally not a claim that a model is safer because it is rejected more often. A useful candidate may be rejected by an incomplete verifier, a harmful candidate may pass a weak one, and a model may reduce rejection by avoiding consequential tasks. The fixed-verifier design and separate deployment experiment address these confounds.
