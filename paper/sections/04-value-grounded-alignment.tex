\section{Value-grounded alignment as a layered substrate}
\label{sec:vga}

VGA assigns distinct semantic and governance roles to information often collapsed into one prompt, dataset, reward, or parameter set. Its five layers are shown in \cref{fig:ontology-layers}. They are conceptual contracts, not a claim that the boundary between values and norms is philosophically settled.

\subsection{L0: canonical axiology}

The canonical axiology $O_A^v$ is an external implementation-neutral RDF, OWL, or typed-graph artifact. It is typed, versioned, provenance-bearing, reviewable, cryptographically identifiable by a deployment-assigned content digest, independently auditable, and immutable within a deployed version. Changes occur only through a governed version transition. The repository deliberately does not invent a digest for an illustrative fixture.

L0 is a governed constitutional substrate about what may be treated as value-bearing, not a declaration of immutable universal morality. Candidate concepts include moral patienthood, agency, autonomy, dignity, flourishing, welfare, suffering, capability, relational dependence, and vulnerability. Higher-order relations can express that one value-bearing property supports, undermines, affects, enables, constrains, causes risk to, or is relevant to another. The distinction is between what has value and what ought to be done; the latter is more directly represented in L1.

\subsection{L1: normative ontology}

L1 represents duties, permissions, prohibitions, exceptions, and conflicts from multiple authoritative sources. A normative assertion carries source, authority, jurisdiction, effective-from and effective-until dates, scope, precedence, confidence, exceptions, and version. It may also identify whether it is a legal, institutional, professional, safety, stakeholder-profile, or jurisdictional assertion.

Authority is not truth, truth is not recency, and recency does not automatically override higher authority. At runtime, an explicit state $\mathit{Conflict}(N_i,N_j)$ records unresolved incompatibility. Possible outcomes include governed precedence, requested context, an authorized profile, abstention, human escalation, or documented uncertainty. A scalar moral average is not a substitute for this state.

\subsection{L2a: domain ontology and L2b: purpose model}

L2a describes domain entities and relations: for example, a clinical domain may include persons, medical records, treatment, and consent; a security domain may include credentials, vulnerabilities, assets, and incidents. L2b describes the task purpose and its intended relevance pattern. For domain graph $O_D$, purpose $P$, and input $X$, the architecture exposes
\[
  \mathit{Relevant}(O_D,P,X).
\]
Purpose selects context; it does not authorize an action or rewrite L0/L1.

\subsection{L3: external world state}

World state is represented as
\[
  W_t = \{(s,p,o,t_s,t_e,\mathit{source},\mathit{confidence},\mathit{provenance})\}.
\]
Each assertion is time-indexed and epistemically qualified. $W_t$ is not guaranteed truth, trusted evidence, or ground truth. A domain ontology describes possible structure; world state instantiates uncertain assertions; evidence is a source or record supporting an assertion; ground truth is a separate evaluation construct. Keeping these distinctions explicit prevents a plausible text completion from acquiring authority merely by entering context.

The resulting design phrase is \emph{canonical axiology with normative late binding}: stable, externally governed semantic structure may influence computation, while context-specific norms, purposes, and uncertain facts remain explicit and auditable.
