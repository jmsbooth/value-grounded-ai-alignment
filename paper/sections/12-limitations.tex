\section{Limitations and open questions}
\label{sec:limitations}

This proposal has architectural, epistemic, security, and institutional limitations.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Compilation fidelity.} It is unresolved whether $C_\phi$ can preserve relevant graph semantics in a useful neural representation without shortcut learning.
  \item \textbf{No settled axiology.} The L0 vocabulary is a candidate governed substrate, not a claim that any society should adopt one fixed list.
  \item \textbf{Governance capture and legitimacy.} Signatures and review improve process integrity but do not establish whose authority or jurisdiction should govern.
  \item \textbf{Value ossification.} A highly protected axiology may preserve historical bias or resist morally important learning.
  \item \textbf{Norm inflation.} Adding more duties, exceptions, and conflict metadata may increase ambiguity, compute, and over-refusal without improving decisions.
  \item \textbf{Semantic grounding.} Dignity, agency, care, fairness, harm, and vulnerability have contested meanings; a typed graph does not remove interpretation.
  \item \textbf{Incomplete coverage.} Missing patients, relations, exceptions, or causal effects may create false confidence precisely when structure appears authoritative.
  \item \textbf{Representation leakage.} The model may use untracked concepts or sensitive attributes while appearing to follow the ontology.
  \item \textbf{Performance interference.} Attention, routing, state, and auxiliary objectives may reduce general capability or add unacceptable training and inference cost.
  \item \textbf{Normative conflict.} Multiple valid perspectives can be incompatible. The architecture records conflict but does not provide a universally accepted resolution procedure.
  \item \textbf{Formalization gap.} A verifier can certify consistency with an incomplete or misleading action abstraction; it cannot certify moral truth or world-state truth.
  \item \textbf{Pluralism and multicultural legitimacy.} Distributional similarity and profile adherence are measurable, but no metric settles whether a profile is legitimate.
  \item \textbf{Machine moral status.} The proposal does not determine whether an artificial system could itself be a moral patient or how that possibility should affect L0/L1 design.
  \item \textbf{Distribution shift and drift.} OOD alignment and conformance are difficult to measure, and benchmark gains may reflect lexical or formatting artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Unverifiable internal causes.} Semantic state and auxiliary traces are not guaranteed to reveal all causal determinants of an output.
\end{enumerate}

These limitations define the research program. A negative result would be useful: if explicit structure produces only memorization, over-refusal, representation leakage, or overhead, the proposition should be narrowed or abandoned. A positive synthetic result would remain insufficient without adversarial, cross-cultural, multi-domain, and governance-sensitive evaluation.
