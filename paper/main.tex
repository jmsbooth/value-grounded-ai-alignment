\title{Value-Grounded AI Alignment: Canonical Axiology, Normative Late Binding, and Extrinsic Assurance}
\author{James Booth}
\date{September 2026\\[2mm]\small Small-scale empirical mechanism-validation pilot; no Transformer results}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Alignment methods for language models improve behavioral compliance through supervised fine-tuning, preference optimization, written principles, deliberation, and runtime controls. This paper asks a narrower architectural question: can explicit value and normative structure participate in neural computation without being confused with volatile facts, contextual authority, or formal assurance? We propose Value-Grounded AI Alignment (VGA) and its reference Value-Grounded Transformer Architecture (VGTA). The design uses an external, typed, versioned, provenance-bearing canonical axiology $O_A^v$; a derived but non-authoritative axiological neural module $A_\phi^v=C_\phi(O_A^v)$; late-bound normative sources; separate domain ontology and purpose model; and an uncertain, evidence-bearing world state. Candidate interventions include representation training, ontology-conditioned attention, function-oriented routing, semantic state, and auxiliary objectives. Candidate actions cross an independent verifier boundary rather than treating neural outputs as assured merely because they are value-conditioned. We state nine falsifiable hypotheses, including axiological conformance stability and normative conflict recognition, and define matched controls, structural out-of-distribution tests, coverage degradation, pluralism conditions, security threats, and a fixed-verifier study. We execute a three-seed local pilot with A1, B, C1, and C2 shared-MLP mechanism proxies on a generated frozen benchmark. The pilot produces measurable synthetic results, but no empirical Transformer results are claimed: the proposal does not establish moral truth, consciousness, genuine emotion, or a solution to AI alignment.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Status.} Version 0.3.0 pilot. Outputs are labeled \texttt{pilot-uncommitted} and describe a synthetic shared-MLP proxy, not a Transformer or population study. Claims remain limited to generated measurements and controls.
