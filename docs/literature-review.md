# Literature review

**Review date:** 2026-09-07  
**Scope:** primary papers, proceedings, recognized philosophical works, and standards relevant to the proposed VGA architecture. Recent preprints are marked as such and are not treated as settled evidence.

The review supports a constrained novelty claim. Knowledge-enhanced Transformers, graph-conditioned computation, value-conditioned alignment, runtime safeguards, and symbolic verification all have substantial prior art. The open question is whether the particular division of computational role and modification authority proposed by VGA yields measurable benefits under matched, adversarial evaluation.

## Recent 2025--2026 monitoring

The review specifically checked recent work on value principles, normative conflict, pluralistic alignment, and reward hacking. The 2025 ACL paper by Xu et al. studies hierarchical value principles and scenario-specific retrieval; Millière's 2025 paper analyzes normative conflicts; Yaacov's 2025 preprint proposes pluralistic deliberation; and Çağatan and Zhao's 2026 preprint revisits reward hacking in language-model agents. These sources strengthen the case for explicit evaluation and conservative novelty language, but none establishes VGTA or the VGA combination. The 2026 item is an arXiv preprint and should be rechecked before a final publication version.

## Review conclusion

The literature supports the following bounded conclusion: explicit knowledge and value structures, contextual principles, modular updates, deliberation, and symbolic checking each have precedent. VGA's remaining research contribution is the testable combination of (i) a stable axiological prior, (ii) context-sensitive normative late binding, (iii) external purpose and world state, (iv) structural participation in Transformer computation, (v) differentiated modification authority, and (vi) independent assurance. The combination is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated novelty or safety guarantee.
